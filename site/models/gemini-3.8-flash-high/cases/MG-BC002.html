Because the quadratic term 12∥⋅−z∥2\frac{1}{2}\|\cdot - z\|^2 is continuously differentiable with gradient p−zp - z, the subdifferential sum rule applies: 0∈∂r(p)+(p−z)  ⟺  z−p∈∂r(p).0 \in \partial r(p) + (p - z) \iff z - p \in \partial r(p). By definition of the Fenchel subdifferential of rr at pp, for all u∈dom ru \in \text{dom } r: r(u)≥r(p)+⟨z−p,u−p⟩.(3)r(u) \ge r(p) + \langle z - p, u - p \rangle. \qquad (3) We now rewrite the inner product ⟨z−p,u−p⟩\langle z - p, u - p \rangle using the Euclidean polarization identity: ∥u−z∥2=∥(u−p)+(p−z)∥2=∥u−p∥2+∥p−z∥2+2⟨u−p,p−z⟩.\|u - z\|^2 = \|(u - p) + (p - z)\|^2 = \|u - p\|^2 + \|p - z\|^2 + 2\langle u - p, p - z \rangle.
