本题成绩

题目与要求

请独立解答以下题目。可使用本地工具进行计算、验证和保存自己的工作；不得访问网络或进行联网检索，也不得访问参考答案、评分、已有答卷或其他对话。

I am preparing the mathematical appendix and a short deployment description for an autonomous denoising block. The state is z=(x,y) in R², and

f(x,y) = (x²+2y²)/2 + (x+y)^4/4.

Write Φ_t(z) for the exact solution at time t of ż = −∇f(z), starting at z. Our block is T_τ=Φ_τ, with a configurable τ>0; stacking n identical blocks gives Z_n=T_τ^n(Z_0). This is the exact continuous-flow block, not an implicit-Euler discretization.

Here is the design rationale in the current draft: “The vector field is the gradient of a strongly convex energy, and its evolution is contractive and dissipative. Thus, for every τ>0, the exact block has a convex proximal representation T_τ=prox_{r_τ} for some proper lower-semicontinuous convex r_τ. This interpretation makes each deployed block an exact convex regularization step.” We use the convention

prox_r(z) = argmin_u {r(u) + ||u−z||²/2}.

Please supply the appendix and then a short deployment paragraph:

  1. Establish forward global well-posedness, identify the equilibrium, and derive ||Φ_t(z)−Φ_t(w)||≤e^(−t)||z−w|| and f(Φ_t(z))≤e^(−2t)f(z). Deduce quantitative convergence for a stack.

  2. State and prove the standard proximal certificate used in the rationale: if a map T admits T=prox_r with r proper, lower-semicontinuous and convex, then, for p=T(z) and every u in dom r,

r(p) + ||p−z||²/2 + ||u−p||²/2 ≤ r(u) + ||u−z||²/2.

Also derive firm nonexpansiveness under that representation.

  1. Give a concise mathematical description of what the actual continuous-flow block and its repeated deployment guarantee, suitable for the methods section. Distinguish the evolution time τ from the coefficient in our definition of prox. A closed-form solution of the ODE is not needed.

The contraction and dissipation claims are correct, but the draft’s proximal conclusion is false. For this energy, no positive-time flow map Φτ\Phi_\tau is the proximal map of a proper lower-semicontinuous convex function.

Write A=(1002),b=(11). A=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&2\end{pmatrix}, \qquad b=\binom11. Then ∇f(z)=Az+(b⊤z)3b,∇2f(z)=A+3(b⊤z)2bb⊤⪰I. \nabla f(z)=Az+(b^\top z)^3b,\qquad \nabla^2f(z)=A+3(b^\top z)^2bb^\top\succeq I. Thus ff is 11-strongly convex, with unique minimizer and equilibrium 00, and f(0)=0,f(z)≥12∥z∥2. f(0)=0,\qquad f(z)\ge \tfrac12\|z\|^2.

The vector field is smooth, so local existence and uniqueness hold. Along a solution, ddtf(z(t))=−∥∇f(z(t))∥2≤0. \frac{d}{dt}f(z(t))=-\|\nabla f(z(t))\|^2\le0. Consequently, the solution remains in the compact sublevel set {u:f(u)≤f(z(0))}\{u:f(u)\le f(z(0))\}. The continuation theorem therefore gives a unique solution for every t≥0t\ge0. The flow depends smoothly on its initial condition.

Strong convexity gives ⟨∇f(a)−∇f(c),a−c⟩≥∥a−c∥2. \langle\nabla f(a)-\nabla f(c),a-c\rangle\ge\|a-c\|^2. Applying this to two trajectories yields ddt∥Φt(z)−Φt(w)∥2≤−2∥Φt(z)−Φt(w)∥2. \frac{d}{dt}\|\Phi_t(z)-\Phi_t(w)\|^2 \le-2\|\Phi_t(z)-\Phi_t(w)\|^2. Hence ∥Φt(z)−Φt(w)∥≤e−t∥z−w∥. \boxed{\|\Phi_t(z)-\Phi_t(w)\|\le e^{-t}\|z-w\|.}

For the energy estimate, strong convexity evaluated at 00 implies f(z)≤⟨∇f(z),z⟩−12∥z∥2≤12∥∇f(z)∥2. f(z)\le \langle\nabla f(z),z\rangle-\tfrac12\|z\|^2 \le\tfrac12\|\nabla f(z)\|^2. Therefore ddtf(Φt(z))≤−2f(Φt(z)),f(Φt(z))≤e−2tf(z). \frac{d}{dt}f(\Phi_t(z))\le-2f(\Phi_t(z)), \qquad \boxed{f(\Phi_t(z))\le e^{-2t}f(z).}

Uniqueness gives the semigroup identity Φs∘Φt=Φs+t\Phi_s\circ\Phi_t=\Phi_{s+t}. Thus an exact stack satisfies Zn=Φnτ(Z0),∥Zn∥≤e−nτ∥Z0∥,f(Zn)≤e−2nτf(Z0). \boxed{ Z_n=\Phi_{n\tau}(Z_0),\qquad \|Z_n\|\le e^{-n\tau}\|Z_0\|,\qquad f(Z_n)\le e^{-2n\tau}f(Z_0). } It also obeys ∥Tτn(z)−Tτn(w)∥≤e−nτ∥z−w∥. \|T_\tau^n(z)-T_\tau^n(w)\|\le e^{-n\tau}\|z-w\|. In particular, every input converges exponentially to 00, the unique fixed point of TτT_\tau.

Now suppose, conditionally, that T=prox⁡rT=\operatorname{prox}_r, where r:R2→(−∞,+∞]r:\mathbb R^2\to(-\infty,+\infty] is proper, lower-semicontinuous and convex. For p=T(z)p=T(z), the minimizer’s optimality condition is z−p∈∂r(p). z-p\in\partial r(p). Consequently, for every u∈dom⁡ru\in\operatorname{dom}r, r(u)≥r(p)+⟨z−p,u−p⟩. r(u)\ge r(p)+\langle z-p,u-p\rangle. Combining this with ∥u−z∥2=∥u−p∥2+∥p−z∥2+2⟨u−p,p−z⟩ \|u-z\|^2 =\|u-p\|^2+\|p-z\|^2+2\langle u-p,p-z\rangle proves the requested certificate: r(p)+12∥p−z∥2+12∥u−p∥2≤r(u)+12∥u−z∥2. \boxed{ r(p)+\tfrac12\|p-z\|^2+\tfrac12\|u-p\|^2 \le r(u)+\tfrac12\|u-z\|^2. }

For q=T(w)q=T(w), adding the subgradient inequalities at pp and qq gives ⟨(z−p)−(w−q),p−q⟩≥0. \langle(z-p)-(w-q),p-q\rangle\ge0. Thus TT is firmly nonexpansive: ∥T(z)−T(w)∥2≤⟨T(z)−T(w),z−w⟩. \boxed{\|T(z)-T(w)\|^2 \le\langle T(z)-T(w),z-w\rangle.} Equivalently, ∥T(z)−T(w)∥2+∥(I−T)(z)−(I−T)(w)∥2≤∥z−w∥2. \|T(z)-T(w)\|^2+ \|(I-T)(z)-(I-T)(w)\|^2\le\|z-w\|^2.

To determine whether these proximal conclusions apply to the actual block, we need an additional necessary property: a continuously differentiable convex proximal map has a symmetric Jacobian. Indeed, define ψ(z)=sup⁡u{⟨z,u⟩−r(u)−12∥u∥2}. \psi(z)=\sup_u\left\{\langle z,u\rangle-r(u)-\tfrac12\|u\|^2\right\}. Its unique maximizing point is T(z)T(z). Comparing maximizers at zz and z+hz+h, and using the Lipschitz bound supplied by firm nonexpansiveness, gives 0≤ψ(z+h)−ψ(z)−⟨T(z),h⟩≤⟨T(z+h)−T(z),h⟩≤∥h∥2. 0\le\psi(z+h)-\psi(z)-\langle T(z),h\rangle \le\langle T(z+h)-T(z),h\rangle \le\|h\|^2. Hence ∇ψ=T\nabla\psi=T. If TT is C1C^1, then DT=∇2ψDT=\nabla^2\psi is symmetric.

For the given flow, fix t>0t>0 and consider zε(s)=Φs(ε,0),Jε(s)=DΦs(ε,0). z_\varepsilon(s)=\Phi_s(\varepsilon,0), \qquad J_\varepsilon(s)=D\Phi_s(\varepsilon,0). Variation of constants gives, uniformly for 0≤s≤t0\le s\le t, zε(s)=(εe−s0)+O(ε3). z_\varepsilon(s)=\binom{\varepsilon e^{-s}}0+O(\varepsilon^3). The variational equation is J˙ε=−[A+3(b⊤zε)2bb⊤]Jε,Jε(0)=I. \dot J_\varepsilon =-\left[A+3(b^\top z_\varepsilon)^2bb^\top\right]J_\varepsilon, \qquad J_\varepsilon(0)=I. Since (b⊤zε(s))2=ε2e−2s+O(ε4), (b^\top z_\varepsilon(s))^2 =\varepsilon^2e^{-2s}+O(\varepsilon^4), another application of variation of constants yields Jε(t)=e−At−3ε2∫0te−A(t−s)e−2sbb⊤e−As ds+O(ε4). J_\varepsilon(t) =e^{-At} -3\varepsilon^2\int_0^t e^{-A(t-s)}e^{-2s}bb^\top e^{-As}\,ds +O(\varepsilon^4). In particular, (Jε(t))12=−ε2e−t(1−e−3t)+O(ε4),(Jε(t))21=−3ε2e−2t(1−e−t)+O(ε4). \begin{aligned} (J_\varepsilon(t))_{12} &=-\varepsilon^2e^{-t}(1-e^{-3t})+O(\varepsilon^4),\\ (J_\varepsilon(t))_{21} &=-3\varepsilon^2e^{-2t}(1-e^{-t})+O(\varepsilon^4). \end{aligned} Subtracting, (Jε(t))12−(Jε(t))21=−ε2e−t(1−e−t)3+O(ε4). \boxed{ (J_\varepsilon(t))_{12}-(J_\varepsilon(t))_{21} =-\varepsilon^2e^{-t}(1-e^{-t})^3+O(\varepsilon^4). } For every fixed t>0t>0, this is nonzero when ε≠0\varepsilon\ne0 is sufficiently small. Thus DΦtD\Phi_t is not everywhere symmetric, ruling out every claimed convex proximal representation.

Finally, τ\tau is the duration of evolution. The quadratic coefficient in the stated proximal convention remains 1/21/2. Introducing a proximal step size through the energy would give prox⁡τf(z)=arg⁡min⁡u{τf(u)+12∥u−z∥2}=arg⁡min⁡u{f(u)+∥u−z∥22τ}. \operatorname{prox}_{\tau f}(z) =\arg\min_u\left\{\tau f(u)+\tfrac12\|u-z\|^2\right\} =\arg\min_u\left\{f(u)+\frac{\|u-z\|^2}{2\tau}\right\}. Its optimality equation is u+τ∇f(u)=z, u+\tau\nabla f(u)=z, which defines an implicit-Euler step. It does not define the exact flow block.

A suitable deployment paragraph is:

We use the exact time-τ\tau solution map of the autonomous gradient system z˙=−∇f(z)\dot z=-\nabla f(z). For every τ>0\tau>0, the block is globally defined, is e−τe^{-\tau}-Lipschitz, and satisfies f(Tτz)≤e−2τf(z)f(T_\tau z)\le e^{-2\tau}f(z).
