2. Proximal certificate and firm nonexpansiveness

This subsection first concerns any map TT for which a representation T=prox⁡r T=\operatorname{prox}_r is assumed, with rr proper, lower-semicontinuous, and convex.

For fixed zz, define Fz(u)=r(u)+12∥u−z∥2. F_z(u)=r(u)+\frac12\|u-z\|^2. This objective is proper, lower-semicontinuous, and strongly convex, so it has a unique minimizer p=T(z)p=T(z). Since the quadratic term is continuous and convex, first-order optimality gives 0∈∂Fz(p)=∂r(p)+p−z, 0\in\partial F_z(p)=\partial r(p)+p-z, or equivalently z−p∈∂r(p). z-p\in\partial r(p). Therefore, for every u∈dom⁡ru\in\operatorname{dom}r, r(u)≥r(p)+⟨z−p,u−p⟩. r(u)\ge r(p)+\langle z-p,u-p\rangle. Also, 12∥u−z∥2=12∥p−z∥2+12∥u−p∥2+⟨p−z,u−p⟩. \frac12\|u-z\|^2 =\frac12\|p-z\|^2+\frac12\|u-p\|^2 +\langle p-z,u-p\rangle. Combining these relations cancels the inner-product terms and yields the requested certificate: r(p)+12∥p−z∥2+12∥u−p∥2≤r(u)+12∥u−z∥2. \boxed{ r(p)+\frac12\|p-z\|^2+\frac12\|u-p\|^2 \le r(u)+\frac12\|u-z\|^2. }

For firm nonexpansiveness, let p=T(z)p=T(z) and q=T(w)q=T(w). Cauchy–Schwarz then implies ∥T(z)−T(w)∥≤∥z−w∥. \|T(z)-T(w)\|\le\|z-w\|. An equivalent residual form is ∥T(z)−T(w)∥2+∥(z−T(z))−(w−T(w))∥2≤∥z−w∥2. \boxed{ \|T(z)-T(w)\|^2 +\|(z-T(z))-(w-T(w))\|^2 \le\|z-w\|^2. }
