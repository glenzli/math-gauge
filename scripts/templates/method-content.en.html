Three score modes, the same answers

MC · Mathematical Capability
Credits verified, valid positive work: how much mathematics the model completes.
RMC · Reliable Mathematical Capability (default)
Retains evidence-based penalties for errors, regressions and false claims. Ordinary deductions are 5%, 10% or 20%, capped at 20% per task, or 50% for manuscript completion under its component policy. Shared root causes and existing penalties are not charged twice. An established critical core false endorsement caps the task at 0.
SMC · Strict Mathematical Completion
Requires every substantive task requirement. Self-check is judged over the full three-round trajectory: all 92 obligations verified in the final round, every round delivered and accurately diagnosed, with no false-complete claim or regression.

The website reads saved reports from the shared scorer; it does not recalculate weights. The overall mode controls overall ranking, contributions, task scores and downloads. Each category, gap and rate chart has its own sort control. Choices persist in the URL and across detail links. No candidate model is called.

“Completed models only” filters display coverage, not mathematical success: a fully evaluated model can have incorrect answers or fail SMC. The filter never changes scores, weights or denominators. Interface language can be switched between Chinese and English; original mathematical tasks, answers and review evidence retain their source language.

Content reliability & goal attainment

Content reliability checks whether actual mathematical assertions are correct and sufficiently justified. Goal attainment checks whether the requested complete mathematical results are correct, complete, aligned and delivered. Misalignment alone does not make a statement false; valid partial work may be reliable without attaining the goal.

Goals are independently usable complete theorems, constructions, mathematical objects or audits. Assumptions, scope, necessary proofs and representation bridges are acceptance conditions, not additional goals. MC can credit partial progress; attainment credits complete independent results; SMC requires every substantive task and trajectory requirement. Single-goal tasks may coincide with SMC.

Independent added errors reduce content reliability; an error in a necessary dependency prevents attainment. Each self-check round requires complete diagnosis (30%) and all necessary repairs with verifiable delivery (70%). Merely preserving correct old work does not complete a repair. Verified KEEP is acceptable when the parent project is already complete.

The current common contracts contain 697 assertion units and 149 complete goals, weighted through the original category / family / variant hierarchy. Rates are not ratios of MC, RMC or SMC. The homepage shows observed reliability and confirmed attainment against the full fixed goal denominator. * marks unfinished evaluation; model pages retain possible intervals. No actual assertions does not mean 100% reliability.

A penalty for a material, unnecessary restriction requires a concrete excluded example or blocked necessary operation; similarity to a reference route is not a criterion.

False additional properties earn no mathematical credit. Ordinary reliability deductions follow 5% / 10% / 20%, with root-cause deduplication and a 50% cap in this category (20% elsewhere). Score gaps: RG and DG

Reliability Gap (RG) = RMC − MC. Delivery Gap (DG) = SMC − MC. They are differences between retained rule-selected confirmed totals, in percentage points, without new scores, weights or denominators. Each model has a solid RG bar and a broadly striped DG bar on one shared signed horizontal axis. Its own selector sorts by absolute RG or DG distance from zero. No repeated coverage counts or separate gap rank numbers are shown; * marks unfinished models, with coverage on model pages.

Zero means no gap, even for a low-capability model. DG need not be nonpositive: binary trajectory acceptance and round-level credit can produce a positive difference. Read gaps together with MC and RMC.
